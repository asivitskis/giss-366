Lab 6#

ArcGIS Experience Builder#

GISS/GEOG 366/368 · Web Mapping & Web GIS#

How this lab works: The hands-on building happens in Esri’s free, self-paced course, ArcGIS Experience Builder Basics. This lab page does not repeat Esri’s instructions. It tells you what to complete, in what order, what to notice while you work, and what to submit. There is no Thursday class this week, so plan to work through this on your own schedule (see the pacing guide below).

Shortened cycle: Lab 6 uses a shorter version of the Ask, Collect, Visualize, Create, Act cycle: Ask (a quick prediction), Build (the Esri exercises), and Act (submit and reflect).

Step 1: Ask#

Before you open the Esri course, write down your first-pass answers. Do not look anything up. You will revisit these in your reflection.

  • Think back to the apps you explored in Tuesday’s session. List two features you saw that you would have had to hand-code in Leaflet or MapLibre.

  • Predict: how many minutes will it take you to complete the full set of exercises from start to finish? Which exercise do you think will be the hardest, and why?

  • Pick one thing about hand-coding a map (Labs 3 to 5) that you expect the builder to make easier, and one thing you expect it to take away from you.

Lab 6 Question: In Labs 3 to 5, you controlled every line of what your map did. Today you will give some of that control up. Before you start, what do you think you will gain and what do you think you will lose?

Step 2: Build#

Part A: Confirm access#

Sign in to your ArcGIS Online organizational account and open the Esri course using the link on the Week 8 page. Confirm that you can reach the first exercise and that Experience Builder opens from your account. If either fails, post in the discussion board right away so the answer helps everyone.

Part B: Complete the Esri exercises, in order#

The seven exercises build on each other, and you will keep working on one single experience from start to finish. Do them in order, and do not delete or restart your experience between sittings. The times below are the estimates Esri lists in the course; give yourself extra time if you are new to Experience Builder.

Working rules:

  • Follow Esri’s directions and naming conventions for your experience exactly. I will use the name to find your item in our organization.

  • Save as you go. If you leave and come back, reopen the same experience from the Experience Builder home page.

  • Use the course’s built-in “view result” and “show answer” prompts to check yourself. They are for you; I do not collect them.

  • If an exercise does not behave the way the instructions say, treat it as a troubleshooting moment: re-read the previous step, check the documentation, and then ask. Keep a note of what you tried, because you will describe one of these moments in your reflection.

Part C: Notice as you work. Click to expand

Keep these prompts in mind as you work. Jot notes if that helps, and nothing here is collected. They feed straight into your reflection questions.

  • Exercise 1: Look at how the course frames the app’s purpose and audience before building anything. How is that different from how you started Labs 3 to 5?

  • Exercise 2: Watch for places where the builder makes a choice for you. Where could you override it, and where could you not?

  • Exercise 3: Pay attention to what happens in one widget when you do something in another. How would you have wired that up in code?

  • Exercise 4: Notice who the added help or information is for. What would a first-time visitor miss without it?

  • Exercise 5: Compare theme settings to the CSS you wrote in Lab 3. What stayed the same in spirit, and what is gone?

  • Exercise 6: Notice how many decisions about different screen sizes you had to make, and which ones the builder handled for you.

  • Exercise 7: Test the published app the way a stranger would. What did you only notice after publishing?

Step 3: Act#

What to turn in (graded: Lab Exercises)#

  1. The link to your published experience (the final exercise shows you how to get it).

  2. Confirmation that your experience is shared with the organization, so that your instructor can open it and see your work. If you are unsure how to check, use the sharing guidance in the final exercise, and tell me in your submission if something stopped you.

  3. A short written reflection (~400 words total, roughly 60 to 80 words per question) answering:

    • Hand-built vs. builder-built: Pick one behavior in your finished app (for example, something that updates when a user selects something else). Describe how you would have built the same behavior in Leaflet or MapLibre, and name one thing the builder made easier and one thing it decided for you.

    • Workflow: Which step of the Experience Builder workflow did you spend the most time on, and which did you underestimate? Use the step names from Tuesday’s session.

    • Widgets and actions: Using one widget you configured as your example, explain the difference between its content, style, and action settings. Say whether one behavior in your app was a message action or a data action, and how you could tell.

    • Reach and access: Who can open your published experience right now? What has to be true about the web map and data behind it for that to work? Name one accessibility or device decision you made or saw in the exercises, how you tested it, and one barrier a real audience could still face.

Submit your experience link and written reflection to this week’s submission space in Canvas.

Separate assignment: The Week 8 discussion (finding and sharing a published ArcGIS experience in a field you care about) is graded separately. It builds on Tuesday’s gallery exploration, and what you learn building your own app here will help you evaluate the one you choose.

Lab 6 AI Policy: Level 2#

This lab permits optional use of AI to support student learning. Permitted uses can include using AI to help troubleshoot a step that is not working in Experience Builder or to explain a term from the Esri course in plainer language. However, independent installation and use of AI agents to complete GIS tasks is not permitted at this time. AI may also be used to support general writing procedures (outlining a response, improving clarity or grammar, etc.), but your reflection must remain original to you as the sole responsible author, and it must describe your own experience building your own app. Disclosure of AI use is not required for this Level 2 assignment. Using AI is a choice with ethical considerations, and it is asked that students weigh those considerations accordingly as they complete this work.
