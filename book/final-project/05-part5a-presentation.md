# Part 5a: Presentation

**Due:** Week 15 · **Points:** 50 · **Format:** Slide deck + peer review

This week is about testing whether your project reads clearly to someone outside your head. Select rubric items for this deliverable are co-designed with the class — watch for that conversation in the Week 15 lecture.

## Peer review exchange

Before you present, you'll exchange your near-complete webmap/app (from Part 4, Step 4) and draft report sections with a classmate for structured feedback. Come to the peer review ready to answer, and to ask:

- What's confusing about this map to someone seeing it for the first time?
- Does the interactivity/styling actually communicate the result, or just decorate it?
- Is there a claim in the Discussion section that isn't backed by the Results?

Bring the feedback you receive into your revisions for Part 5b.

## Building your slide deck

Your presentation is a condensed, visual version of your report — not a read-aloud of it. Aim for roughly one slide per section:

1. **Question & study area** — what you're investigating and why here
2. **Audience & significance** — who this is for, why it matters
3. **Data & methods** — what you collected, how, and what analysis tools you ran (name your stack: AGOL, FOSS, or mixed)
4. **Live demo or screengrab** — show the actual webmap/app, not just a static image if you can help it
5. **Results** — your key charts/patterns, described, not yet interpreted
6. **Discussion & significance** — what it means, and what you'd do next with more time/resources
7. **Ethics note** — briefly name who your map reaches, who it doesn't, and what your architecture choice cost your audience

Keep text minimal — slides support what you say, they don't replace it.

## Delivering the presentation

You'll present for **8–10 minutes** to the class, live webmap/app included where possible. Practice the transition into your demo so it doesn't stall the room if a service is slow to load — have a screengrab backup ready.

## Submitting Part 5a

- Slide deck (PDF or link)
- A short written note on what feedback you received in peer review and how you plan to act on it
